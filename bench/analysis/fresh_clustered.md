## 2. THE headline inference — commit-clustered paired bootstrap, D vs E

On the post-repair full-100 set (D 42/100 = 42.0%, E 30/100 = 30.0%):

> **RD (D − E) = +0.12 (+12pp), 95% CI [-0.049, +0.271], two-sided bootstrap p = 0.192** (commit-clustered paired bootstrap, 44 clusters, B=10,000, seed 20260802).

This is ONE coherent paired inference: the percentile interval and the
percentile-inversion p-value are read off the same resampling
distribution, so they cannot disagree about alpha=.05, and the
resampling respects the commit-level dependence the reviewer flagged.
Suggested paper sentence: *"D exceeded E by 12 percentage points, but
the commit-clustered matched comparison was inconclusive at this sample
size (95% CI [-0.049, +0.271], p = 0.192)."*

**Why clustering widens this contrast** — the advantage is concentrated:
of D's net +12 paired-task advantage over E, **+7 comes from a single commit** (`87a6eccfb8c6`, the 9-task AntitoneOn sum–integral family, where D went 7/9 and E 0/9) and +3 from `49ed1b2de10c` (the 8-task bounded-variation file, D 4/8 vs E 1/8) — i.e. 83% of the net effect sits in 2 of 44 commits, which is exactly the pseudoreplication the reviewer flagged.

### Old numbers (supplement only — replaced by the above)

- Paired-Wald RD 95% CI: [+0.0015, +0.2385] (barely excludes 0).
- Exact McNemar (38 discordant pairs): p = 0.0730.
- Why they disagreed: The paired-Wald interval is a normal approximation over all 100 paired differences while exact McNemar conditions on only the 38 discordant pairs, and near the significance boundary these two approximations can land on opposite sides of alpha=.05 — which is why v2 replaces both with a single commit-clustered bootstrap whose interval and p-value come from the same resampling distribution.
